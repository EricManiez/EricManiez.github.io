Trained at **École 42** in Paris, the tuition-free peer-learning coding
school founded by Xavier Niel. The entry point is the **Piscine**: a
month of full-time C and Unix immersion designed to reveal who can
actually stick with the discipline.

The rest of the cursus follows the same principle — no lectures, no
professors. Just a progression of projects (shell, algorithms, systems,
networking), each corrected by peers before a machine has the final
word. You learn to read other people's code as much as to write your
own, and you get very comfortable being the least experienced person
in the room.

What I took away: a hard baseline in C and Unix, the habit of
debugging without a safety net, and the conviction that the best way
to learn something is to ship a working version of it — then have
someone else pick it apart.
